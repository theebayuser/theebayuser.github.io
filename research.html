§ 01 · Lemma

Every line, checked

a page for SAT math, written by hand

Most practice tools only look at the answer. Lemma looks at every line of the work.

The student writes on an iPad with a Pencil, the way they would on paper. A box around the answer hands the page in. A symbolic kernel then marks each line: does it follow from the line before, and is the boxed line the answer? After that, the student names the reason for each legal line, and the kernel checks the reason too.

No language model gives a verdict. A model can read the handwriting, one line at a time, but the kernel (Python and SymPy) decides what is true.

Vocabulary

Two words worth defining

Everything on this page is built out of these.

Definition 1 (warrant).

The named rule that makes a step legal. For a step on both sides of an equation, the student writes the operation itself, such as

+ 20    ÷ 5

and the kernel checks that this operation really turns the line before into this line.

Definition 2 (kernel).

The program that decides what is true. It compares each line with the line before it and with the problem. A line that restates the problem, or checks a value such as

4 · 13 − 11 = 41

is not an error, even when it does not follow from the line above. The rules are written as tests, so each mark is the same every time.

If 5x − 20 = 15, what is the value of x?

✗ 5x = −5 does not follow20 subtracted, not added
✓ 5x = 35 + 20same step on both sides
✓ x = 7 ÷ 5same step on both sides, and the answer
