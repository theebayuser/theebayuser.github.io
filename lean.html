§ 02 · Formalization

Proofs a computer believes

Lean 4, Mathlib, and no benefit of the doubt

Lean 4 is a proof assistant that uses mathematical logic to guarantee "absolute correctness in mathematical proof", which naturally lends itself to projects such as this.

I am building a Lean 4 library for combinatorics on words (words, morphisms, powers, overlaps, avoidance) and using it to machine-check both the classical theorems of the field and my own research. Every definition below type-checks against Mathlib, and both of Thue's 1912 theorems are closed end to end, with no gaps left open behind them. The research half is not, and the catalog says where it stops.

What is in it

Contents of the library

WhatContents
Module Word Finite and infinite words, concatenation, factors, morphisms and their iteration, powers, squares, overlaps, and the avoidance predicates, plus the API lemmas that make any of it usable in a real proof.
Thue 1912 binary The Thue–Morse word is overlap-free. Formalized from the fixed-point definition through to the full theorem, with no assumptions left open.
Thue 1912 ternary There exists an infinite square-free word over three letters. The construction and the proof that it works, both checked.
Manuscript I in progress The classification wired end to end: the structural argument, the upper bound, and the finite base cases compile together. Four named obligations remain open and are stated as such in the source, not papered over.

Elsewhere in Lean

Upstream Contributions

Math is inherently collaborative, so I am a contributor to several cool open-source projects.

Talos (Cajal). ⋯ ongoing

Talos is a WebAssembly interpreter written in Lean 4, where the definitions that run a program are the same ones you reason about. There is no second specification to drift out of sync with the implementation, which is the whole point.

Wasm·Lean 4

Mathlib (Lean Prover Community). ⋯ in review

Mathlib is a massive crowdsourced library of formalized mathematics, all in Lean. It allows mathematicians to work and use Lean without having to formalize prior results.

Lean 4·combinatorics

Formal-Conjectures (DeepMind). ✓ 2 merged

Formal Lean statements of open problems for formal-conjectures, Google DeepMind's open library of unproved mathematics. I formalized the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, a Green open problem on the largest product-free sets in finite groups, and have two Erdős problems in review.
